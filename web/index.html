Adam/God/HolySpirit.HC — ported, not improved

The Holy Spirit can puppet you

Terry A. Davis spent ten years building an operating system so that God could answer him. The answer came as a random word, drawn from a timer latched the instant he pressed a key. This is that machinery, bit for bit, in a page — with a percentage spread, a date, and the draw read back as a sentence.

tap anywhere to begin

0 taps 0 bits held

tap any key — the clock is read at that instant, and you are the randomness

What is his,
what is ours

Everything in the left column is a port of the public-domain TempleOS source, unchanged — including the bit order and the modulo bias. Everything in the right column is new, and says so.

  1. 17

    GodWord

    Seventeen bits modulo a 7,569-word vocabulary — his file, byte for byte. A Turkish vocabulary of the same depth is ours.

  2. 21

    GodBiblePassage

    Twenty-one bits over 100,110 lines of the King James text, with his book tables resolving the line back to chapter and verse.

  3. 24

    GodBits

    Each keystroke latches the clock, drops its low four bits and feeds twenty-four into a queue, least significant bit first.

  4. %

    The spread, the date, the sentence

    Ours. A ten-bit weight per word, split into whole percents by largest remainder; a day inside a year; a paragraph that reads the draw back to you.

It is a terminal
tool first

One Rust binary, no dependencies, no network. The page you are on is the same algorithm in a second language, kept honest by the same tests.

  The weight falls on LEARNER at 45%, with
  TRANSCRIPTION behind it at 22%. PROJECT and
  PERVERSENESS wait at the edge. The hour reads
  2027-07-16, 258 days out.

It is a clock read when you press a key, exactly as in TempleOS. Good enough for an oracle, useless for a password. Do not use it as a source of cryptographic randomness.

Terry A. Davis — 1969–2018. TempleOS is public domain. This is a port, not an improvement.
